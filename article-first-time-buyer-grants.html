First Time Home Buyer Grants and Programs in 2025

By the MortgageWise Team  ·  9 min read  ·  Updated January 2025

← Back to Calculator

Here's something a lot of first time buyers don't know: there's a significant amount of money out there specifically designed to help people like you buy their first home. Grants that don't need to be repaid. Loans with 0% interest. Programs that cover your entire down payment. The catch is you have to know where to look — and most people don't.

This isn't "too good to be true" stuff. These programs are funded by federal, state, and local governments specifically because homeownership builds long term wealth and stable communities. They want people to buy homes. Here's what's actually available.

Federal Programs Available Everywhere

Federal Program

FHA Loans — 3.5% Down with Flexible Credit

Not a grant but worth mentioning first because it's the most used first-time buyer program in the country. FHA loans are backed by the government and only require 3.5% down with a 580 credit score. The trade-off is mortgage insurance that lasts the life of the loan, but for many buyers it's the most accessible path to homeownership.

Federal Program

VA Loans — 0% Down for Veterans

If you're an eligible veteran, active duty service member, or surviving spouse, VA loans are genuinely one of the best financial products in existence. Zero down payment, no PMI, competitive rates, and no minimum credit score set by the VA. If you qualify for this, it's almost always the best option available to you.

Federal Program

USDA Loans — 0% Down in Eligible Areas

USDA loans offer zero down payment financing for homes in eligible rural and suburban areas. More areas qualify than people think — it's not just farmland. Check the USDA eligibility map at usda.gov to see if the area you're looking in qualifies. Income limits apply.

Federal Program

Good Neighbor Next Door — 50% Off for Public Servants

Teachers, law enforcement officers, firefighters, and EMTs can buy HUD-owned homes in revitalization areas at a 50% discount through this program. The catch is you have to live in the home for at least 3 years. Search available properties at hud.gov/goodneighbor.

State and Local Programs — Where the Real Money Is

This is where most people leave money on the table. Every state has a Housing Finance Agency (HFA) that runs its own first-time buyer programs. These can include:

Grant

Down Payment Assistance Grants

Many states offer outright grants — free money that doesn't need to be repaid — to cover some or all of your down payment. These are typically 3–5% of the purchase price and are often targeted at buyers below certain income thresholds. You don't need to be broke to qualify — many programs go up to 120% of the area median income.

Assistance Loan

Forgivable Second Mortgages

Some programs provide a second mortgage to cover your down payment that gets forgiven if you stay in the home for a certain number of years — typically 5–10. Live there long enough and you never pay it back. These are common in states like California, Texas, Florida, and New York.

Assistance Loan

Deferred Payment Loans

Similar to forgivable loans but you do eventually repay them — just not until you sell the home or refinance. Since you pay nothing monthly, it doesn't affect your debt-to-income ratio and doesn't add to your monthly payment burden while you're living there.

How to Find Programs in Your State

The fastest way is to search Google for: "[your state] first time home buyer assistance 2025"

Or go directly to your state's Housing Finance Agency website. Every state has one — just search "[your state] housing finance agency."

You can also ask any mortgage lender about down payment assistance programs — good lenders know what's available in your area and can help you stack programs to maximize your benefit.

💡 Stack Multiple Programs: You can often combine programs. For example, an FHA loan plus a state down payment assistance grant plus a local homebuyer education incentive. A good mortgage broker who specializes in first-time buyers will know how to stack these for maximum benefit.

What You'll Typically Need to Qualify

  • Must be a first-time buyer (usually defined as not owning a home in the last 3 years)
  • Income below a certain threshold (varies by program and area)
  • Minimum credit score (varies — often 620–640)
  • Complete a homebuyer education course (usually free online, takes 4–8 hours)
  • Purchase a primary residence within the program's eligible area
  • Home price below program limits (typically tied to area median home prices)
