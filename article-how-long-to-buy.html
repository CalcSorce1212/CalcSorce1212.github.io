How Long Does It Take to Buy a House?

By the MortgageWise Team  ·  8 min read  ·  Updated January 2025

← Back to Calculator

One of the first questions people ask when they decide to buy a home is "how long is this actually going to take?" The honest answer is it depends — but most people are surprised by how long the full process really is when you factor in everything from getting your finances in order to the day you actually get the keys.

For most buyers the complete journey takes somewhere between 4 and 12 months. Here's how that breaks down step by step.

The Full Timeline

1
1–3 Months

Getting Your Finances Ready

This is the part most people skip or rush — and it's the most important. Checking your credit score, paying down debt, saving up your down payment and closing costs, and getting a clear picture of your monthly budget. If your credit needs work this phase can take 6–12 months on its own.

2
1–2 Weeks

Getting Pre-Approved

Once your finances are in order, getting pre-approved is actually pretty quick. You submit your documents to a lender — pay stubs, tax returns, bank statements — and they typically give you a decision within a few days to a week. Shop at least 3 lenders before settling on one.

3
1–6 Months

House Hunting

This is the phase with the widest range. In a hot market with low inventory you might be searching for months before you find something — and then lose it in a bidding war and start over. In a slower market you might find the right place in a few weeks. Most buyers tour 10–15 homes before making an offer.

4
1–5 Days

Making an Offer and Negotiating

Once you find the right home your agent helps you put together an offer. The seller responds — accepting, rejecting, or countering. This back and forth usually wraps up within a few days, though in competitive markets you might be making offers on multiple homes before one gets accepted.

5
30–60 Days

Under Contract to Closing

Once your offer is accepted the clock starts. You'll have your inspection, the lender orders an appraisal, underwriting reviews your full file, and title insurance gets sorted. This phase has a lot of moving parts and 30–45 days is typical, though some closings stretch to 60 days.

What Can Slow You Down

A few things that commonly extend the timeline beyond what buyers expect:

  • Credit issues — if your score needs improvement before you qualify, add 3–12 months
  • Low inventory markets — in competitive cities buyers sometimes search for 6+ months
  • Appraisal problems — if the home appraises below purchase price you'll need to renegotiate
  • Underwriting delays — lenders get backed up especially in busy seasons
  • Title issues — occasionally old liens or ownership problems need to be resolved
  • Inspection negotiations — if major issues are found and the seller pushes back it can add weeks

What Can Speed It Up

  • Getting fully pre-approved before you start looking — not just pre-qualified
  • Having your documents organized and ready to submit quickly
  • Working with an experienced local agent who knows the market
  • Being flexible on closing date — sellers sometimes accept lower offers for a convenient timeline
  • Avoiding new credit applications or major purchases during the process
💡 The Most Common Mistake: Starting to look at homes before your finances are ready. Getting emotionally attached to a house you then can't get approved for is genuinely painful. Sort out your pre-approval first — then start the fun part.

The Realistic Answer For Most People

If your credit is good and your down payment is saved, most buyers close on a home within 3–4 months of seriously starting the process. If you're starting from scratch with credit work and savings needed, 9–12 months is more realistic. Either way, the process moves faster when you're organized and working with good people.

Know Your Numbers Before You Start

The fastest way to speed up your timeline is knowing exactly what you can afford before you talk to a single lender or tour a single home.
